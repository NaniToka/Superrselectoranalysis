⚡ Why Delivery-Level i.i.d. Sampling Fails
  • Non-Zero Covariance: Balls within a match share pitch wear, dew, and chase pressure: Cov(εit, εjt | Match m) ≠ 0.
  • Flawed Assumption: Naïve delivery-level sampling assumes independent errors, artificially shrinking standard errors by ~45%.
  • Block Resampling: We enforce 200 match-level bootstrap resamples, resampling entire match histories (M = 851) to preserve sequence covariance.
📐 Monte Carlo Convergence & Bounded Error
  • Estimator Formulation: Selection probability P̂i = (1/B) ∑ 𝕀(Ranki(b) ≤ 10) across B = 200 resamples.
  • Bounded Standard Error: SE(P̂i) = √[P̂i(1 - P̂i)/B] ≤ 3.54% across all 202 qualified players.
  • 95% Wilson Confidence: Invariant core anchors (P̂ ≥ 75%) maintain statistically unassailable separation from lower tiers.

Section F: Limitations & Nuance
Methodological Boundaries, First-Innings Asymmetry & Score Tier Interchangeability
Ranks 4 to 10 Tier Clustering: Model scores between Rank 4 (0.438, Babar Azam) and Rank 10 (0.322, Harry Brook) form a narrow band. The exact sequence shifts across resamples; Rajasthan Royals should treat them as an interchangeable cohort.

Known Model Limitations

  • Innings Asymmetry: 2nd innings (chase) win probability model achieves AUC 0.89 (Brier score 0.137) with high confidence. 1st innings model achieves AUC 0.68 due to target unobservability and pitch variance.
  • Batter-Bowler Matchups: batter-bowler matchups are not modelled (opposition quality enters only through a leave-one-match-out rating).
  • Small-Sample Volatility: Players with 150-250 balls carry larger bootstrap confidence intervals than 500+ ball veterans. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage penalizes small samples, but tournament-to-tournament variance remains.

While one possible explanation is that opposing batters adopt an unusually risk-averse approach against him, the underlying cause (e.g. batter risk aversion vs. match state vs. execution) is untested in the data pack.
